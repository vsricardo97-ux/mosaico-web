WOD format glossary

The abbreviations on the whiteboard come up again and again. Here are the most common ones, how to read each, and what to log as your result.

AMRAP
As Many Rounds (or Reps) As Possible. A fixed time, say 12 minutes: you do as many rounds or reps as you can. Logged as “6 rounds + 12 reps”.
For Time
A fixed amount of work, as fast as possible. You log the time, for example 3:58. It usually has a time cap.
Time cap (TC)
The maximum time to finish a For Time. If you hit the cap, you log the reps you had left.
EMOM
Every Minute On the Minute. At the start of each minute you do the assigned work and rest for whatever is left. You log whether you finished every minute and with what load.
Rounds for time (RFT)
A fixed number of rounds, say 5, of a list of movements. The lowest time wins.
Chipper
A long list of movements with lots of reps that you “chip away” at from top to bottom, once.
Tabata
20 seconds of work and 10 of rest for 8 rounds: 4 minutes. You log the lowest round or the total reps, as your coach indicates.
21-15-9
A descending rep scheme: 21 of each movement, then 15, then 9. Fran is the best-known example.
1RM
One-rep max: the heaviest weight you move for a single rep. “5RM” is the max for five. Logged in kilos or pounds.
RX
As prescribed on the board: the same weights, reps and movements.
Scaled
The adapted version, with a lighter load or an easier movement. It’s a way to train at the right intensity, not a failure.
Buy-in and cash-out
A block done before (buy-in) or after (cash-out) the main part of the WOD.
