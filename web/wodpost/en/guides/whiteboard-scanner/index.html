WOD whiteboard scanner

A box whiteboard packs a lot into a small space: warm-up, strength, the WOD, time caps, weights for men and women. A whiteboard scanner turns that photo into an organised workout, without you typing it. It isn’t only for whiteboards: a screenshot from your training app or a photo of a computer screen works too.

What a whiteboard scanner does

It reads the text in the photo and gives it structure: it separates the parts of the class and identifies the format (AMRAP, For Time, EMOM…), the list of movements, the reps and the loads. The result is no longer an image but a workout you can save, search and compare with another day.

What it usually reads well

  • Clear marker or chalk handwriting, even with the odd scribble.
  • Common formats: For Time, AMRAP, EMOM, rounds, chippers and strength sets.
  • Barbell weights (“43/30 kg”) and rep schemes like 21-15-9.
  • Screenshots from your box’s app or site.

What to double-check

A good scanner doesn’t fake certainty. If the photo has glare, is crooked or the writing is cramped, check the numbers: one misread load changes the workout. Wodpost flags in orange what it isn’t sure about so you can tap it and fix it in seconds.

How to take a good photo

  1. Stand square on to the board, no angle, so the lines don’t bend.
  2. Avoid the glare of the lights: take a step until it disappears.
  3. Get the whole board in, but close enough to read every line.
  4. If your box’s app already has the workout, a screenshot works too.

After it reads the board

With the WOD read, you add your time, rounds or 1RM and Wodpost builds the card for your story, over your workout photo or video. The workout stays in your library and, if you beat a mark, the card says so. How the photo and data are handled is explained in the privacy policy.
